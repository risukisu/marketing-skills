# Ad Creative - Thought Leader Ads, Conversation Ads & Copy

How to write the ads. Remember: the format serves the strategy. Pick the
strategy first (see `references/strategy-and-measurement.md`), then use these
formats to execute it. When you draft copy, scrub AI-writing tells - the
`copy-deslop` skill is good for this, and thought leader ads especially must read
like a real human posted them, because that authenticity is the whole point.

## Thought Leader Ads (TLAs)

A TLA boosts a *personal* post (an employee's, founder's, customer's, or
partner's) rather than a company-page post. The person approves a request, then
you sponsor their post. Why they work: they're native - they don't look like ads,
so people engage with them as normal posts. The result is consistently higher
CTR, higher engagement, and **lower CPM** than company-page ads.

**Format rules / gotchas:**
- Images and video are supported; **carousels/documents are not** (may change).
- **Max 4 emojis** - LinkedIn ad policy won't let you spend behind a post with
  more. Edit the post down if needed.
- You can **add a link in the post** to drive direct clicks (you're sponsoring it,
  so you don't need to rely on organic reach) - useful for events/webinars.
- The voice can be anyone with approval: founder, internal SME, employee,
  customer, or partner/affiliate. *Who* says it is a creative lever - test the
  same message from a CEO vs an SME vs a customer.

**Where to put the CTA: in-post link vs first-comment.** Two valid patterns -
pick by how transactional the offer is and how much you want the post body to
read like a normal LinkedIn post:

- **In-post link** is right when the CTA *is* the value proposition (a webinar
  registration, an ebook), and a sponsored post can carry the link without the
  organic reach penalty.
- **First-comment CTA** is right when the post is primarily about insight or
  story and you want the body to read as native, non-promotional content. The
  poster publishes the post, then drops the link as the first comment ("PS - if
  you want the full breakdown..."). Preserves authenticity, often gets higher
  engagement, and the comment is visible right under the post. Especially good
  for "I'd love to compare notes / DM me / here's the resource if you want it"
  framings where a link in the body would break the voice.

### The 14 TLA plays (idea bank)

Use these to brainstorm; each is a "container" you can run many creatives from.

1. **Announce new features / integrations / partnerships** - speak to experts as
   experts; show what they can achieve.
2. **Highlight the core problem you solve + your solution** - after reading, the
   prospect should be able to articulate the problem and your fix. If they can't,
   simplify the message.
3. **Case study featuring your own company** - "we used our own tool to get X."
   Practicing what you preach is powerful proof.
4. **Customer case study / results** - third-party outcomes.
5. **Generate buzz before an in-person event** - ABM list of attendees + job
   functions; drive awareness pre-event.
6. **Stay top of mind after an event** - recap/insights post-event.
7. **Promote your own event** - speakers, sessions, your talk.
8. **Promote an upcoming webinar** - the value, what they'll learn.
9. **Promote your newsletter** - simple, steady audience-builder.
10. **Celebrate customer wins** - awards, outcomes; social proof.
11. **Demo product use-cases in-feed** - an SME records a short demo of a specific
    use case; you get people consuming a "demo" at a fraction of the cost.
12. **Share long-form insight your ICP cares about** - don't fear long copy if
    it's valuable; break down a genuine insight or analysis.
13. **Practical advice from your own platform's data** - mine your unique data for
    insights nobody else can publish ("when our data contradicts the consensus,
    here's what we see"). Highly credible.
14. **Social proof from existing customers** - a customer posts about their win on
    *their* page, in front of *their* network; you boost it. The most believable
    proof there is.

## Conversation Ads

A message placement that lands in the prospect's LinkedIn inbox. You build a
branching flow - set multiple response buttons that route to different next
steps. Strong for **direct response**, and best in **remarketing** (warm
audiences), where it tends to beat image/video on conversion because you can give
context and guide the choice.

- Supports the `%FIRSTNAME%` personalization macro (also works in Spotlight/Text
  ads). You can study competitors' conversation ads in the **LinkedIn Ad Library**
  by searching "first name" + an offer term like "demo."
- Structure: a short, human opener from a named sender → 2-3 branch buttons (e.g.
  "Show me how it works" / "Send me the case study" / "Not now") → each leading to
  a relevant asset or a booking link. Keep it conversational, not a pitch wall.

## Writing the copy - B2B services principles

- **Lead with the problem, not the product.** Problems are the gateway into the
  account. "How to fix your [specific painful thing] in 10 minutes" earns the
  click; a feature list doesn't.
- **Be specific and felt.** Narrow problems your ICP is frustrated about *this
  week* beat broad "important" themes that get a nod and no action.
- **Bridge problem → solution cleanly.** Good example pattern (Apollo TLA): open
  with the felt pain ("nobody got into sales to do data entry"), then bridge to
  what you free them to do. The reader should finish able to state the problem and
  your solution in one breath.
- **Offer, not just ask.** On cold traffic, lead with a no-brainer offer (see
  strategy ref), not "book a demo." Reposition the meeting around the outcome
  ("architecture review," "workflow teardown"), not the format.
- **One story, repeated.** Tie creatives back to the persona's messaging story so
  everything compounds instead of scattering.

## Output format when drafting ads

When asked to write ads, produce something the user can paste and run:
- State the **strategy + objective + audience + format** in one line at the top
  so the creative is anchored to a plan, not floating.
- For a **TLA**: give the suggested **voice** (whose post), the **post body**, an
  optional **in-post link + CTA line**, and note the ≤4-emoji rule if relevant.
- For a **conversation ad**: give the **sender**, **opener message**, and the
  **branch buttons** with where each leads.
- Offer **2-3 variations** (matching the "3 ads per campaign" guidance), varying
  the angle/hook, not just wording.
- Keep it human; run a deslop pass. Save to a file if the user will review/reuse.
