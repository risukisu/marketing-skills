## Why this part exists

People who make content make links. The goal of collaboration is to be visible
to the small group of people in your field who publish: editors, writers,
podcasters, event organizers. Not the big social accounts; a large following on
a platform earns no search authority if the person has no site of their own.
Relevance beats reach, and smaller, highly relevant creators answer their email.

The framework article cites a figure of 18% of B2B brands running ongoing
influencer programs. Orbit's survey shows collaboration frequency tracks with
reported results: those who collaborate usually or always report strong results
at more than twice the rate of those who never do. Collaboration also improves
the content itself, adds credibility, and gives every piece a co-promoter. An
ally in making the content is an ally in sharing it.

You are not asking for links. You are making friends with the people who give
them. Some will link. That's the long game.

## Method

### Find the right people

Look for the ones who publish: bloggers, journalists, columnists, podcast
hosts, event organizers in your niche. Sort candidates by relevance first, site
authority second. Aim for five to ten named people per quarter.

### Warm up before asking

Follow, read, share with a comment, reply with a point of view. Then move to a
real conversation: a call beats an email, a handwritten note beats a thank-you
email. The bar is low because almost nobody does this.

### Five free formats

Offer before you ask. Include them in your content first.

1. **Contributor quote.** While writing, ask one person for a short quote on a
   specific question with a specific deadline. Include their name, face, link.
2. **Round-up.** Send the same three or four questions to a handful of people.
   Write it as journalism: summarize, compare, add your analysis. Not eighty
   people pasted into one page.
3. **Interview.** Email a series of questions to one person, or record a call
   and publish video plus an edited transcript. Easier for them than writing.
4. **Host a guest post.** Invite a relevant creator to write for your site.
5. **Pitch a guest post.** Offer to write for theirs. Editors get pitched
   constantly; relationship first, pitch second. Details in part 7.

### Etiquette

Show contributors at their best: a line about their achievements, a link to
their site, a note when it goes live with a ready-to-share summary. End every
message with an open offer to collaborate on anything; it plants the idea for
podcasts, quotes, and guest slots they might have.

## Checklist

- [ ] Five to ten named people who publish, relevance-ranked
- [ ] Warm-up started (follow, share, comment) two weeks before any ask
- [ ] Formats chosen for this quarter (at least a quote and one interview or round-up)
- [ ] Research contributors doubled as collaborators
- [ ] Notify-and-thank step scheduled for each live piece

## Questions to ask the user

- Who does your audience already trust and read? Who publishes in your niche?
- Which of the five formats fit this quarter's hub?
- Who have you quoted, hosted, or been quoted by before?
- Who could you get on a call in the next two weeks?
