## Why this part exists

When you publish a number nobody else has, every writer who covers the topic
has to cite you, and citing means linking. That makes original research the
single most link-worthy thing a company can publish. The framework article
estimates it costs about ten times the effort of a normal article and returns
about a hundred times the result. Orbit's own annual blogger survey has been
their most-linked and most-shared piece year after year, and it reports that
roughly half of content programs now publish some research (49% in the 2025
edition, up from a quarter in 2018). Still rare enough to stand out, common
enough that "we can't do that" is no longer credible.

Proof is easy to find: open Search Console's links report, or any SEO tool's
top-pages-by-links view, for any company that has ever published a study. The
study is usually at the top.

## Method

### Scoping: story plus data

Research is a story supported by numbers, not a spreadsheet dump. Before
collecting anything, write the headline you hope to publish and the one or two
sentences that state what the study is about. If you can't, the topic isn't
ready.

Pick a topic that:
- your audience argues about or guesses at,
- fits what the company wants to be known for,
- nobody has measured, or nobody has measured recently or for this niche.

Ways to find one: claims you make to clients anecdotally and wish you could
prove; questions clients ask that you can't answer with data; a niche adjacent
to a crowded topic (not "email marketing", but one specific practice within
it). Search `"[topic]" research [year]` and `"[topic]" benchmarks` first to see
what exists.

Size it to finish inside one quarter. One headline finding and five supporting
charts beats a forty-page report nobody reads.

### Three data routes

1. **Survey.** Design the questions so each one produces a chart you'd want to
   publish; cut any question you can't picture in the report. Keep it short.
   Test it on a handful of people before sending. You need a list to send it to:
   your own subscribers, a partner's list, a community, or a paid panel.
2. **Dataset you can assemble.** Public filings, job postings, pricing pages,
   directory listings, your own anonymized customer data. Often cheaper than a
   survey and harder for others to replicate.
3. **Structured interviews.** Twenty to fifty short conversations with the same
   question set. Slow, but yields quotes and stories along with counts.

### Publishing

Give the research a deliberate home: a comprehensive article with the charts
and method, plus a short summary post, plus optionally a gated deeper version
(the content upgrade). Include a method note: sample size, dates, how collected.
Add contributor quotes from a few recognized people in the field; they bring
credibility and they share it when it goes live.

### Promotion

Research is the center of the quarterly hub. From it come: the infographic, the
how-to article that cites it, the guest posts that cite it, the influencer
interview that discusses it, and the podcast pitch. Tell everyone you quoted.
Tell everyone who has written about the topic before. Repeat annually if it
works; a recurring study compounds.

## Checklist

- [ ] Headline written before data collection starts
- [ ] Topic passes: audience cares / fits the brand / not already measured
- [ ] Data route chosen and list or dataset access confirmed
- [ ] Every survey question maps to a chart
- [ ] Sample target and dates set; finishable this quarter
- [ ] Two to four contributors lined up for quotes
- [ ] Home decided: full article + summary + optional gated version
- [ ] Method note drafted
- [ ] Outreach list drafted: people quoted, prior writers on the topic

## Questions to ask the user

- What does your audience wish someone would measure?
- What do you tell clients as an anecdote that you'd love to prove with data?
- What data can you reach that competitors can't: customer base, partner list,
  public records in your niche?
- Who in the field would you want quoted in it, and who would cite it?
- Can you finish it in a quarter? If not, what's the smaller version?
