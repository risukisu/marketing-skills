# Part 7: PR focus, writing for other sites

Distilled from Andy Crestodina / Orbit Media. Sources:
https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/content-strategy-framework/ (part 7),
https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/ (guest posting figures),
https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/increase-domain-authority/. Own words
throughout.

## Why this part exists

Research and relationships may produce links. Writing for someone else's site
almost always does: at minimum in the author bio, at best in the body, pointing
at your research. For a young program with low authority, this is the fastest
lever there is, and it also puts you in front of an audience you don't have
yet.

The comparison from the framework article, restated: two marketers each write
eight articles. One publishes all eight at home. The other publishes four at
home, pitches four elsewhere, and invites four outside writers in. The second
ends the quarter with more links, more relationships, and a wider audience,
from the same writing effort. Orbit's survey finds only about 37% of bloggers
ever pitch guest posts, and those who do report strong results at twice the
rate of those who don't.

## Method

### Target list

Five to ten sites your buyers actually read, that publish outside
contributors, and whose authority is at or above yours. Check they link to
authors and don't mark all links nofollow. Trade publications, partner blogs,
association sites, and niche newsletters all count. Podcasts count too: show
notes link.

### The pitch

Short. Relationship first if you can (see part 6). Then one paragraph:

- who you are and why you're relevant to their readers,
- two or three title options, one of them the evil twin of your best how-to,
- a one-line summary of the angle and the new thing it brings (your research
  is the new thing),
- a link to one published piece as a writing sample.

Offer an infographic plus a short original article when a plain article might
be refused. Editors like ready-made visuals.

### Deliver and follow through

Write to their style guide, on time, original (never republish your own post).
Cite your research in the body where it genuinely supports the point; that's the
link that matters. Bio link to the service page or the research hub. When it's
live, share it, thank the editor, and send it to the sales team; a byline on a
respected site is proof for prospects too.

### Hosting the other direction

Inviting outside writers costs you editing time and gains you their promotion,
their perspective, and a relationship that often returns the favor. Hold guests
to the same mission and quality bar as your own posts.

## Checklist

- [ ] Five to ten target sites listed with editor contact and link policy
- [ ] At least two pitches sent per quarter, each with two or three titles
- [ ] One evil-twin piece and one infographic-plus-article offered
- [ ] Research cited in the body of every guest post where it fits
- [ ] Live pieces shared, editor thanked, sales notified
- [ ] One hosted guest post per quarter

## Questions to ask the user

- Which five sites would you most want to appear on? Do your buyers read them?
- What could you pitch them this quarter: the evil twin, the infographic, a
  take on the research?
- Who on the team can write it to deadline?
- Who would you invite to write for you?
