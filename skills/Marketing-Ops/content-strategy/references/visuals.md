# Part 5: Visual formats

Distilled from Andy Crestodina / Orbit Media. Sources:
https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/content-strategy-framework/ (part 5),
https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/ (visuals and video
figures). Own words throughout.

## Why this part exists

Text is the base layer of every successful program. Visuals are the multiplier.
In Orbit's blogger survey, the share of respondents reporting strong results
climbs steadily with the number of visuals per post, from the mid-teens for one
image to around half for seven or more, against a benchmark of about one in
five overall. Most bloggers use one to three images; very few use many. The
survey also finds roughly one in four bloggers now produce video regularly. As
with every rare tactic in this framework, rarity is why it correlates with
results.

Visuals also do a job text can't: an infographic or chart set is what you pitch
to another site when a plain guest post would be refused, and it's the piece
other writers embed with a link back.

## Method

### What to repurpose

Don't make visuals for everything. Make them for the pieces that already earned
attention or that need to travel:

- **The research.** Every headline finding becomes a chart. The chart set
  becomes an infographic. The infographic becomes the guest-post offer.
- **The top how-to.** A process diagram, a checklist graphic, or a short video
  walk-through.
- **The sales page proof.** A comparison table, a before/after, a results chart.

### Format picker

| Format | Use when | Travels as |
|---|---|---|
| Chart set | Research findings, any numbers | Embedded with citation link |
| Infographic | A story with 5-8 data points | Guest-post offer, social, embed |
| Process diagram | A how-to with clear steps | In-article, sales deck |
| Short video (2-5 min) | Explaining something people get wrong | Social, YouTube, embedded in the article |
| Carousel / slide set | Distilled list content | LinkedIn, sales follow-up |

### Production reality

Decide who makes visuals before promising them: in-house designer, contractor,
templated tool. A consistent visual style across the hub matters more than
polish on any one piece. Every visual carries the brand, the source, and a URL.

## Checklist

- [ ] Research charts planned (one per finding)
- [ ] One infographic planned from the research
- [ ] One visual version of the best how-to
- [ ] Sales-page proof visual identified
- [ ] Producer and tool decided; style consistent across the hub
- [ ] Every visual credits source and URL

## Questions to ask the user

- Which existing piece performed best? Which planned piece needs to travel?
- Who can produce visuals this quarter, and with what tool?
- Is video realistic this quarter? If so, one topic, which?
