## Why this part exists

Two kinds of people arrive from search. One group wants to know something
(how, what, why, tips, examples). The other wants to do something (a service
name, "consulting", "provider", "pricing", a city plus a category). The first
group lands on blog posts and leaves without contacting anyone; the source
article reports blog-landing conversion rates in the fractions of a percent.
The second group lands on service pages and converts at a rate that makes the
whole program worth running. Roughly eight in ten searches are the first kind,
one in ten the second, which is why the blog gets the traffic and the service
page gets the leads.

Service pages have a problem, though: nobody links to them. Links go to useful
content. So the service page depends on the rest of the program to lift the
domain's authority high enough to rank, and the program depends on the service
page to turn that authority into leads. Build the page first. If it converts at
zero, nothing else matters.

## Method

### Pick a phrase you can eventually win

1. List the commercial-intent phrases a buyer would type when looking for what
   this page sells. Use the buyer's vocabulary, not internal lingo; one word
   can shift a phrase from research intent to buying intent.
2. Check the site's Domain Authority (DA) in any SEO tool, and the DA of the
   pages that currently rank for the phrase. "Good" DA is relative: it means
   higher than the sites already ranking for your phrase, nothing more.
3. If the ranking pages sit far above your DA, the phrase is a nine-to-twelve
   month target, not a launch target. That's fine. The rest of the strategy
   exists to close that gap. Pick it, and pick a narrower fallback phrase you
   can rank for sooner.
4. Remember what does not raise DA: internal links, social profile links,
   press-release links, comment links. What does: editorial links from other
   sites' content, which is what parts 3, 6, and 7 produce.

### Optimize to rank

- Phrase in the title tag, the H1, the URL, and naturally in the body.
- Headings that answer the questions a buyer of this service has.
- Internal links from relevant blog posts using descriptive anchor text.
- Clean technical basics: fast, mobile-friendly, indexable, schema where it fits.
- One page per phrase. Don't split the same intent across two URLs.

### Optimize to convert

The visitor scans fast, looking for their own words. They slow down when they
find them, gain confidence when their doubts are handled, believe when the
claims are backed, and convert when contacting you looks like a low-cost,
high-value next step. Four jobs:

1. **Answer the top questions.** Am I in the right place, what do you do? Is
   this for me, who do you work with? How do you do it and what's different?
   Who is behind this? Answer the first two high on the page, in plain terms.
2. **Handle objections.** Too expensive, too slow, not for companies like
   ours, our situation is different. Each gets a sentence or a proof point.
3. **Show evidence.** Testimonials, client logos, case studies with numbers,
   reviews, credentials, years and volume, team bios. Put proof next to the
   claim it supports, not in a trophy case at the bottom.
4. **Ask clearly.** Calls to action with verbs that sound easy and valuable.
   Above the fold and at the end.

### Specificity test

Vague pages fail the same way. Check five spots for concrete detail: the H1
(says what you do, not a slogan), the hero image (shows the thing or the
people, not stock abstraction), subheads (each carries information), navigation
labels (say what's behind them), and CTAs (name the action and the payoff).
Names, numbers, dates, examples. Replace every generic phrase with one.

## Checklist

**Rank**
- [ ] Commercial-intent phrase chosen, in buyer vocabulary
- [ ] Current DA recorded; DA of ranking pages recorded; gap stated
- [ ] Fallback phrase chosen if the gap is large
- [ ] Phrase in title, H1, URL, body
- [ ] Internal links from at least three relevant posts
- [ ] Technical basics pass

**Convert**
- [ ] First screen answers "what do you do" and "who is it for"
- [ ] Top five buyer questions answered on the page
- [ ] Top four objections addressed
- [ ] Evidence placed next to each major claim
- [ ] CTA above the fold and at the end, with specific verbs
- [ ] Specificity test passed on H1, hero, subheads, nav, CTAs

## Questions to ask the user

- Which service page should generate leads? What phrase should it rank for?
- What's the site's DA today? What's the DA of the pages ranking for that phrase?
  (Offer to pull DA and difficulty if DataForSEO tools are loaded.)
- What's the conversion rate of visitors who land on service pages versus blog
  posts? (Offer to pull from GA4 if loaded; otherwise ask or mark unknown.)
- Which buyer questions does the page not answer? Which objections does it
  ignore? What evidence exists that isn't on the page?
